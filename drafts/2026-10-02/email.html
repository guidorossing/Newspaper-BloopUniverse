Digger Opens Today With a Rotten Score — Tom Cruise's First in Nine Years
The embargo is lifted and the verdict is a coin toss. The one point of agreement is the man in the fat suit.
By The Bloop Times Editorial Desk

Alejandro González Iñárritu's Digger reaches North American cinemas today, and the full reviews that were held back until Tuesday settled where last week's first reactions pointed: straight down the middle. The film debuted on Rotten Tomatoes on Sept. 30 at 52 percent from 79 reviews — Tom Cruise's first “rotten” score in nine years, since The Mummy in 2017 (15 percent), and the first for Iñárritu as a director, according to Screen Rant's tally., and the number keeps moving.

The split is over the film, not the star. The Guardian called it “an exasperatingly shallow, laborious, self-congratulatory satire”; Variety's Owen Gleiberman wrote simply that “Digger isn't funny.” The London Evening Standard found it “audacious, risky, terrific fun,” and Gold Derby's roundup says even many negative reviews single out Cruise's performance.

  “An exasperatingly shallow, laborious, self-congratulatory satire.” — The Guardian

Warner Bros. itself is projecting a $12–15 million opening, TheWrap reports, against an official $125 million budget (Variety has reported $130 million). Variety's weekend preview has Amazon MGM's Verity, tracking at $30–35 million, winning the weekend. Digger is also Warner Bros.' last release before the Paramount takeover closes next week.

Sources: Rotten Tomatoes, Screen Rant, Gold Derby, The Guardian, Variety, TheWrap · Sept. 29–Oct. 1, 2026

Behind the Scenes · The story you weren't told
The Six-Hour Face: How Tom Cruise Timed His Way Out of the Makeup Chair
Digger's prosthetics took six hours the first time. Cruise decided that was a scheduling problem, not a fact of life.

Turning Tom Cruise into oil tycoon Digger Rockwell took grey hair, a fat suit and a face of prosthetics, from makeup and hair designer Alessandro Bertolazzi and prosthetic makeup designer Kazu Hiro. “The first time we did the makeup, it took six hours,” Cruise has said.

On shoots with roughly twelve-hour days, that is a problem, and Cruise has tied his push to those long days and his wish to sleep. So he measured it. “I had a guy there with a stopwatch, and I'm like, ‘OK, you see that time, six hours? By the time we're done, we're going to get it under an hour,’” he told the hosts of the New Heights podcast, Travis and Jason Kelce, on Sept. 18.

  “If you say no, you can't do it, you will never get there.”

The team was skeptical, and that line was his answer. The method was unglamorous: each prosthetic piece was timed on its own, then the sequence was drilled until the minutes came off, with a chalkboard to track the numbers. The full application eventually dropped below an hour — “they didn't even rush,” he said of the team. A cooling machine handled the sweat under long takes.

“I like going into a system, studying it and trying to figure out how I can do it better, more efficient,” he said on New Heights. On The Graham Norton Show, asked how much of the character was actually him, his answer was shorter: “None of that is me.”

Even critics who dislike Digger keep singling out the performance. The five hours that came off the chair are the part you can't see on screen — which is, in a way, the point of a prosthetic.

Told by Tom Cruise on the New Heights podcast with Travis and Jason Kelce (Sept. 18, 2026) and on The Graham Norton Show. Reported by Variety, Yahoo Entertainment, AOL and Filmofilia; makeup credits per Filmofilia and Dork.

 
Blooper of the Week
Tom Holland Couldn't Keep a Straight Face — And Jake Gyllenhaal Posted the Proof
The film
Spider-Man: Far From Home (2019), dir. Jon Watts
What went wrong
In a serious scene between Peter Parker and Mysterio — as the clip is described, the moment Peter hands over the E.D.I.T.H. glasses Tony Stark left him — Tom Holland kept breaking into smiles and laughter, take after take.
Why they laughed
Holland couldn't hold it, and Gyllenhaal, in the clip, jokes that Holland has “got this.” At one point Holland runs out of the shot entirely, which sets Gyllenhaal off too.
Did it stay in?
No — outtake only. The scene itself is in the finished film, played straight; the laughing takes survive only in the clip.

Gyllenhaal posted the clip to Instagram on Sept. 17, 2019, as the film reached digital, with the caption “Miss my scene partner. #spidermanfarfromhome Out Now on Digital.”

Sources: Floor8, ComicBook.com, CinemaBlend, Epicstream — Jake Gyllenhaal's Instagram post, Sept. 17, 2019. Described from outlet coverage; we have not watched the clip.

 
Improvised or Scripted?
Anne Hathaway's waltz into bed.
Verity (2026) — the scene where Verity does something awful, and the director asks for the happiest person who has ever gotten into bed.

It's the kind of choice that looks designed: a character does something terrible and then waltzes to bed. Surely somebody scripted that. The evidence says otherwise.

IMPROVISED

Hathaway told Hola that director Michael Showalter asked her to “get into bed and be the happiest person who's ever gotten into bed.” Her instinct: “For some reason I thought, what if I waltz my way into bed?” She and Showalter laughed once the shot was done and assumed it was too strange to keep. “And we were wrong! It's in the movie,” she said. The direction was Showalter's; the waltz was hers.

Told by Anne Hathaway to Hola (updated Sept. 24, 2026); also reported by Yahoo Entertainment.

Deleted Scene of the Week
The Halloween Kills Ending That Was Pulled for the Sequel's Sake

Halloween Kills (2021) was meant to end on a scene that was written, shot — and then lifted. Director David Gordon Green told Collider's Steven Weintraub in October 2021: “There's an additional scene that we filmed that was scripted. And actually I think is a pretty brilliant scene.”

The reason wasn't pacing or ratings. It was the next movie. “We ended up lifting it when I became more confident of where we're going to pick up in the next movie,” Green said, because it “didn't feel authentic to where we're going to go.” ComicBook.com reported script pages for it: Laurie learning of Karen's death and taking up a knife, then a phone call with Michael, who answers Karen's phone, ending on a bloody knife. That is the script as reported, not the shot footage.

Green said an extended ending would be on the home release, and Collider reported an extended cut on Blu-ray. We couldn't confirm which editions carry it — check before you buy.

Told by director David Gordon Green to Collider, October 2021; script pages reported by ComicBook.com.

Warner Bros. Printed Its Own Bad Reviews. Here Is the Fortnight That Got It There.

Studios print the praise. On Tuesday, Sept. 29, the morning Digger's review embargo lifted, Warner Bros. posted an ad on its official X account that set raves against pans under the line “Same film, different truth,” then “Decide for yourself.” Filmofilia and World of Reel describe the pairings as “Masterpiece” against “Unpleasant” and “Brilliant” against “Baffling”; TheWrap has “Masterpiece” against “Insufferable.” They also disagree on the exact timing. Either way, the studio chose to run it.

Put the dates in a row. Sept. 22: the London premiere, and IndieWire's three writers publish three irreconcilable verdicts. Sept. 29: Warner Bros. makes that split the campaign. Sept. 30: Rotten Tomatoes opens at 52 percent, and a judge clears Paramount's takeover of the studio. Today: Digger opens against a Warner Bros. projection of $12–15 million. Monday, Oct. 5: the weekend number is reported the day Ynon Kreiz starts at Paramount. Tuesday, Oct. 6: the close Paramount is targeting.

That makes Digger the last release Warner Bros. markets as an independent studio, sold with a pitch that concedes the argument before the audience can have it. Whether it works, we can't tell you: presales aren't public, and the budget is its own argument — $125 million officially, $130 million per Variety, and higher in reports Warner Bros. disputes. Our read, flagged as ours: near its projection, the ad will look like a studio being honest about a divisive film; below it, like the last thing the old Warner Bros. said. We'll have the number next Friday.
