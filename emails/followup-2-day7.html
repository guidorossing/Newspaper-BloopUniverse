What you're missing
Eleven Sections. Nine Minutes. Every Friday.
You've read the free edition. Here's the shape of a real one.

The free edition you got was deliberately missing three things: the news. It stays up permanently, so anything dated in it would rot. The Friday paper has no such problem, and it opens with exactly the sections the free one leaves out.

This is what lands, in this order, every Friday morning:

The running order
The Front Page — the week's biggest movie news, with the angle nobody else led with
The Wire — three updates in three lines, including the Actor of the Week
Behind the Scenes — the long story — one scene, one take, told properly
Blooper of the Week — the film, what went wrong, why they laughed, and whether it stayed in
Improvised or Scripted? — one famous line, settled, with who confirmed it
Did You Know? — three things you didn't know on Thursday
The rotating slot — a deleted scene, a near-miss casting, or where they are now
Coming Soon — cinema and streaming, re-checked every single week
Poll & Guess the Movie — your vote, last week's results, five clues to one film
Insiders Only — one story that never becomes a video
Next Friday — what's already in the works

About nine minutes end to end. Long enough to feel like a paper, short enough that you actually finish it.

And every claim in it carries its source. Not because it's tidy, but because a paper about what really happened on set is worth nothing the moment you can't be sure it's true.
